# CoRe controlled-table construction and acceptance details

This companion records the controlled-table construction and acceptance rules. Experimental inputs and outputs remain those of the frozen study. Section labels refer to the [current manuscript](../latex/main.tex); [archived diagnostics](07-归档敏感性与输出核验.md) give the strength distributions and existing passing-seed comparison.

## Construction contract

O is the original benchmark. W and S retain its rows, error cells, chunks and non-identity values, and transfer errors to one-to-one renamed identity values. W uses English words and S uses syllables. F uses other real records with the same schema and error recipes or formatting rules. Its error counts can differ when recipes cannot transfer or a formatting rule applies to more values. These properties support the comparisons in the manuscript's `sec:conditions`, not a claim that all four tables have identical repair difficulty.

The construction produces 21 new tables: three conditions (F/W/S) at three seeds for each of Hospital and Movies, plus three Beers conditions at one seed, or 9 + 9 + 3. Together with three original-condition tables, these give 24 cleaning inputs. Conflict inputs and the Rayyan audit are counted separately. The surrogates contain about 41,000 fictitious values. Main sources are `code/src/validate_tables.py`, the frozen `CoRe-artifact/data/final/` materials, the paper's `tables/construction.tex`, `tables/movieserrors.tex` and `tables/checks.tex`. The original paragraphs and full table text are hash-bound in the source inventory (SR-060–079).

## Hospital

The original has 1,000 quality-measure rows for 45 hospitals and 509 erroneous cells. Of these, 508 replace a character by `x`; in 96% of them every occurrence of that character is replaced. Column error rates range from 2.3% to 3.9%.

F uses the July 2026 CMS registry. Quality measures are joined with hospital information and converted to the benchmark's 20 columns and formats: lower case, digits-only phone numbers and provider numbers without leading zeros. Whole hospitals are added in provider order, starting with those covered by the benchmark, until 1,000 rows are filled. The table has 34 hospitals, of which 33 occur in O. All quality measures have changed, so these are new rows about largely the same entities. Each column receives the original number of errors, with character substitutions drawn by the original position-weighted rule at newly drawn error locations.

The 296 distinct identity values each map to one fictitious value. S uses 24 syllables and W uses 63 English words, with generic suffixes such as `regional hospital` and `street`. Provider numbers, ZIP codes and phone numbers become random digits. At each original erroneous position, the transferred corruption replaces a character chosen by position with `x`. The small syllable inventory is also the inventory used by the decoding analysis in `sec:commercial`; it was not supplied to the cleaner.

## Movies

The 1,000-row original subset comprises all 190 rows replaced wholesale by Rotten Tomatoes records and 810 other rows sampled without replacement using Python seed 0. The selected rows retain benchmark order and are restricted to ten columns that IMDb public datasets can fill. Two table-wide formatting rules apply in addition. The 1,965 error cells include 379 identity errors. The complete column breakdown remains in `tables/movieserrors.tex`.

For example, *American Ultra* (2015) has Rotten Tomatoes slug `american_ultra` in place of IMDb identifier `tt3316948`, year range `2014 2015 2016`, and Rotten Tomatoes score pair `5.4/10,6/10` in place of IMDb rating 6.3. Credits can add, omit or respell names. These are source differences, not uniformly typographical errors.

### Fresh film selection and formatting

For each original film, the constructor selects an absent film from the September 2026 IMDb snapshot. Candidates are feature films with runtime, genres and at least 1,000 votes, released in the same year or, if necessary, within two years. Selection minimizes log-vote-count distance. Clean rows use the first two directors and writers, the first three distinct actors, and durations such as `99 min`.

Fresh row i corresponds to original row i for applying the 190 source-style error recipes. Slugs, year ranges, durations and ratings are instantiated from the original patterns, and the formatting rules are applied to the new values.

- People recipes record names kept, dropped, respelled or added. Added names come from the fresh film's other columns or other IMDb credits, mirroring the additional names in the other source.
- Title recipes use the shortest sequence of one or two edits from eleven kinds: capitalization, `&`/`and`, accents, punctuation, or added/dropped subtitles, tokens and articles. The error-profile table separately records 12 title edit recipes.
- Genre recipes preserve the list slots and map the fresh film's genres to Rotten Tomatoes vocabulary, for example `Action & Adventure,Comedy` for `Action,Comedy`.
- A recipe that cannot apply to its assigned fresh cell moves to another source-style row able to take it.

Four director and four writer recipes remain inapplicable after relocation. F therefore has 371 identity errors, compared with 379 in O/W/S. At seed 0, F has 2,195 errors overall. Every fresh film has at least 1,000 votes, so the separator rule affects 1,000 rating-count cells rather than 768. These counts describe different fresh records under the same mechanisms, not paired error cells.

### Surrogate names and source-style errors

The surrogates rename 6,018 values one to one, including 4,018 people. W person names combine English words into first and compound surnames; S person names use two syllable words. Titles are generated independently of the real title, rather than retaining its function words, numbers and punctuation. Generic shapes include `The X`, `X of the Y`, `X: Y Z` and `X 2`, sampled at the measured rates: 18.5% initial `The`, 6.1% colon and 2.9% final number. W excludes all words appearing in a real title in the table.

Each transferred title error requires a compatible generated shape, such as an `and` token or a subtitle. Identifiers become unique fictitious IMDb numbers; the slug is rebuilt from the dirty fictitious title in the form of the original slug. This preserves the corruption mechanism without providing a mapping from a fictitious slug to a random reference identifier.

### Recovery evidence

The 105 hour/minute duration errors convert to the reference in 50 cases but not in 55; another 80 duration errors are empty and two are other source differences. A format conversion therefore does not always recover the preferred source value. For years, the middle candidate matches 167 of the 190 references. The manuscript retains this candidate-rule example in `sec:knowledge`.

## Beers

The 1,000-row subset includes all 127 rows with city errors and 873 others sampled without replacement using Python seed 0. Selected rows retain benchmark order. This subset contains 421 breweries, rather than 1,000 independent entities. Its 565 errors comprise 311 percentage suffixes and 254 location cells: a state glued to a city and an empty state cell in each of 127 rows. Correcting the suffix or splitting the location retains the necessary information in the input.

F uses a September 2026 Wikidata query for US beers with alcohol content, style and brewery location. Original beers are excluded, no brewery contributes more than 25 beers, and 1,000 beers from 94 breweries are selected. Corruptions retain the original counts and rendering, including the floating-point representation of percentage values. The original has 421 breweries; F is descriptive in the knowledge analysis because brewery concentration and alcohol-content distributions differ.

Surrogates keep generic words such as `Brewing`, `Company`, `IPA`, `Stout` and `San`, and years. Other words become W-pool or S-syllable words. Numbers retain their lengths; apostrophes remain only for possessives and `O'`. Repeated real values map consistently, including a brewery named for a city. A one-word city remains one word, so a correct generated city does not resemble the city/state-gluing error being tested.

## Acceptance and construction seeds

The six check categories contain 67 structural, 137 error-kind/count, 15 error-strength, 263 naming/leakage, 6 fresh-entity and 18 reproducibility checks, totalling 506. Structural checks distinguish paired surrogates from fresh records. Movies column counts generally have an 85%–115% tolerance, with error-kind constraints; rating-count errors are checked against the formatting rule. Naming checks include bijection, no original value or title-word reuse, pool membership and title-shape similarity against random pairing.

Eighteen tables are rebuilt under two hash seeds and must match stored files byte for byte. The three main fresh tables use saved source snapshots. The experimental tables pass 505 checks. Hospital W at seed 0 has one/two/at-least-three-character replacement shares of 62.1%/31.5%/6.4%, versus 53.9%/29.2%/16.9% in O. Its largest difference is 10.5 percentage points, 2.5 points beyond the eight-point tolerance. Seeds 1 and 2 pass this check. The error positions remain fixed in all three seeds. The [archived sensitivity checks](07-归档敏感性与输出核验.md) give the distributions and condition contrasts separately for the passing seeds. Character-replacement counts describe the corruption profile, rather than a measured model-difficulty scale.

Across construction seeds, surrogates redraw names and applicable transferred-error variants. Fresh tables retain the same clean records and redraw errors and their variants. Thus seed ranges concern naming/error draws, while chunk-bootstrap intervals concern the rows of a fixed construction.
